**A. Stats show their real, current values** (Elliott: "the player has to add up additional points from other sources to work out how much they will damage").

1. **One engine helper:** add a pure helper (e.g. `effectiveStats(s, who, space)` in `src/engine/`). It returns a Pokémon's **current Attack** and **current Strength** and their maximum, with every source applied:
   - Location passives (Mt. Ember +1 Attack, Cerulean Cape +1 Strength);
   - Weather brackets;
   - Held Items (Charcoal, Choice Band, Mystic Water…);
   - "until Supper" effects (X Attack, Flame Charge's React, Flannery, Growlithe's On Summon…);
   - "kept" gains (Rage, Flame Charge, Brock, Charmander's On Attack…);
   - damage taken.

   It must use the same functions combat uses, so the number shown is always the number that resolves; `attackParts` already does this for Attack. Return the parts too, for the Codex and previews.
2. **Board tokens and their hover Full card** show the **current** values in the stat tabs:
   - **Raised above the card's printed value:** the number turns **green** with a small ▲.
   - **Lowered below printed** (Attack) or **damaged** (Strength): **red** with a small ▼.
   - **Unchanged:** the normal colour.
   - **Strength** shows current over maximum when they differ, e.g. a small "5/9", or another way that fits the tab neatly. Choose a form that passes the neat-text test and describe it.
   - **A Location or Weather bonus** counts as raised: a Pokémon on Mt. Ember shows its Attack in green with ▲.
3. **Hand cards** keep their printed values; they aren't in play yet.
   - **When placing:** while dragging or choosing a lane, the summon preview from UX3 shows the values the Pokémon **would have there** (e.g. "Attack 4 ▲ on Mt. Ember").
4. **Hovering a raised or lowered stat** shows a one-line sum: "Attack 9 = 8 + 1 Mt. Ember". The Codex keeps the full breakdown.
5. **Everywhere else:** previews, the React window, the log and the Codex all use these same current values.
6. **The neat-text rule applies:** `cardtext.test.js` renders tokens with raised, lowered and damaged stats, including two-digit values, and fails on any overflow or overlap.

**B. The React window keeps the board visible, and clears the moment you decide.**

1. **No more covering the board.**
   - **The prompt:** "REACT?", the sentence and the countdown move into a **slim bar** across the top of the board.
   - **The React choices:** your React cards, **Let it happen** and the timer move into a **tray docked above your hand**, in Mini or compact size. Hovering one shows its Full card beside the tray.
   - **The board stays fully visible** and readable the whole time: every Pokémon, its current stats and the Locations.
2. **Show the consequences on the board itself**, using `attackPreview` / `effectPreview` (engine functions run on a copy, as in UX3).
   - **The threat:** the opponent's committed action is drawn on the board, with the attacker highlighted and the targeting arrow to the target.
   - **On the target,** a **preview badge** shows the result **if you let it happen**: e.g. "9 → Strength 7 → 0 · Defeated · Opponent +2 Recognition, +$1". It also covers hit-backs, Retaliate and triggers, as the UX3 previews do.
   - **For each React card in the tray,** the outcome **if you play it**: e.g. Dive "Charizard returns to your hand · the attack fizzles"; Surf "heal 2 first: 9 → 9 → 0 · still defeated"; Bubble Beam "Gyarados becomes Spent after its attack".
   - **Hovering a React card** switches the board badge to that outcome, so you can compare them on the board.
   - **For a resolving Strat or an Adapt,** show its effect on every Pokémon it touches in the same way.
3. **The window disappears the instant you decide.** When you choose React, Let it happen or a React target, or the timer runs out, the bar, the tray, the badges and the arrow are removed **at once, in the same frame**. Only then does the presenter play the React (if any) and the action, so you see the whole consequence play out on a clear board.
   - **React targets:** for a React that needs a target, picking the card first highlights the targets on the board (the tray stays only until you click a target), then everything clears.
4. **Unchanged:** the timer, its setting, the Space and Esc keys, and React rules all stay as they are.

**B2. Two rulings Elliott has settled** (11 November 2026: "use your best judgement"). These are the only rule changes in this stage.

1. **Bubble Beam's React was a dead effect:** "the attacker becomes Spent after its attack" did nothing, because attacking already makes a Pokémon Spent. It's now:
   - **React, cost 1:** the attack being Reacted to deals **2 less damage** (minimum 0).
   - **Adapt and Strat stay as they are:** Adapt 2: 2 damage, and it becomes Spent; Strat 3: 3 damage, and it becomes Spent.

   Update the card, its mode rows, its glossary entry, `effectPreview` and the React window outcome ("Gyarados's attack deals 2 less: 9 → 7"), `docs/v4-gameplay-update.md` section 5.1, and `FOUNDATION_ASSUMPTIONS.md`.
2. **A waiting Strat is hidden from the other player until it resolves.**
   - **On the board:** while sealed, the Opponent's Strat shows as a **face-down card back** with an hourglass, on the board edge as now. Its **target is shown**: the targeted Pokémon or Location is marked, with an arrow from the sealed card on hover, so you can still plan around it (e.g. Dive that Pokémon).
   - **Hidden text:** the label, log line, Codex entry and previews say only "a sealed Strat" and its target, never the card's name or effect. The Codex explains what a sealed Strat is.
   - **The reveal:** it flips face up as it resolves, and your React window for it (G1 rule) shows the card in full.
   - **Your own sealed Strats** stay fully visible to you.
   - **The AI** must not read the player's sealed Strat card. It may know that one exists and its target, but never its name or effect. Add a test that the AI's choices are identical whichever card is sealed.
