# Pokémon Legends TCG

A browser version of Elliott's custom card game, played against an AI opponent. Built from scratch one small feature at a time.

## Play

- **Online:** the GitHub Pages link for this repo (Settings → Pages).
- **Locally:** run `python3 -m http.server` in this folder and open http://localhost:8000. (Opening `index.html` directly won't work, because browsers block JS modules from `file://`.)

## Layout

| Folder | What lives there |
|---|---|
| `src/engine/` | Game rules only. No DOM, so the AI and tests use the same rules. |
| `src/engine/cardData.js` | Card definitions as data (Pokémon, Trainers). Replace the placeholders with real cards here. |
| `src/engine/effects.js` | The effect system: what every card effect means, as data. |
| `src/ai/` | The AI opponent's decision-making. |
| `src/ui/` | Drawing the board and handling clicks. |
| `docs/rules.md` | The game's rules, written as they're settled. |
| `assets/cards/` | Card art: `<card id>.jpg`, e.g. `growlithe.jpg`, `professor-oak.jpg`. Cards without a file keep the placeholder look. |

No build step: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules. Working rules for each session are in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Build log

- **Step 0** — project shell: empty board, turn indicator, game log.
- **Step 1** — match setup: Battle Arena, secret Trainer placement, coin flip for initiative, Round 1 Breakfast draw and mulligan. Stops at the start of the Battle Phase.
- **Step 2** — Round loop: Rounds run continuously from Breakfast to Battle to Supper. Breakfast sets Base Energy and Exert and draws 7 (reshuffling the Discard Pile when needed). Battle turns are Pass-only; Supper discards hands. Exert/Exhaust payment logic and Trainer Strength (placeholder 15) are in, tested ahead of use.
- **Step 3** — Pokémon cards and "Play a Pokémon Card": card definitions as data (`src/engine/cardData.js`) with 16 placeholder Pokémon. On your turn, click a Pokémon then an empty space to play it, paying Base Energy first and Exert (after a confirm) for any shortfall. Played Pokémon arrive Spent and become Active at the next Breakfast. The AI plays affordable Pokémon using Base Energy only.
- **Step 4** — Combat: Command an Alert Pokémon to attack an enemy Pokémon or Trainer, with Defence / Special Defence targeting restrictions and damage traded between two Alert Pokémon. Defeated Pokémon go to the Released Pile. Matches end in a win (all 3 enemy Trainers at 0) or a draw (no Pokémon left on either side), with a victory/defeat screen. A full match is now playable to the end.
- **Step 5** — Effect system and Trainers: a data-driven effect system (`src/engine/effects.js`) designed for Trainers, Strategy Cards, Pokémon abilities and Innate Powers. Ash, Misty and Brock replace the placeholder Trainers, with Powers (a Battle Action, once per Round) and zone Passives. Trainers at 0 Strength are removed and their zone falls on that side. The AI now scores its moves instead of choosing at random.
- **Step 6** — Strategy Cards and Innate Powers: the "Play a Strategy Card" Action with Quickdraw, Hypervoice and Recover (mostly card data on the effect system), replacing the placeholder Strategy cards in each deck. Quick Attack (summoned Active) and Agility (can't be targeted by Strategy Cards) on placeholder Pokémon. The AI plays Strategy Cards with basic sense.
- **Step 7** — Pokémon abilities: On Summon, On Attack, On Defend, On Defeated, On Breakfast and On Supper, as card data on the effect system. Resolution now runs through one stack, so chained abilities resolve immediately, simultaneous triggers follow the initiative/zone order rule, and an ability can pause for your target choice. Five placeholder abilities on 13 placeholder Pokémon.
- **Step 8** — Supporter Cards: the "Play a Supporter Card" Action, paid in cards (Discard and/or Release) with a Gift Bonus Challenge checked by a general card-matching condition. Tracey, Professor Oak and Mallow replace the placeholder Supporters. One Supporter per Round.
- **Step 9** — Locations, quests and XP: the "Play a Location Card" Action (no Energy; one Location space per zone, replacing allowed), quests as data (summon / attack / Power events, optional filter and zone) that give XP and resolve an effect when completed. Weather Tokens are counted and shown (effects come next). XP is spent at Breakfast on Pokémon in hand, in secret like the mulligan.
- **Step 10** — Weather effects: Rain and Sun as data, with brackets at 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 tokens ("instead" / "also"). Continuous stat effects on both sides (Attack floored at 0, switching off never defeats), and timed effects at Breakfast and Supper that resolve before same-moment abilities. The Weather panel shows counts, active brackets and effects; Weather-modified stats are tagged on cards.
- **Step 11** — Items, PokéDollars, the Bag and the PokéMart: PokéDollars earned for every opponent Pokémon defeated; the "Open Bag" Action (buy from hand then use or store, buy from the shared PokéMart with In Delivery, or use from the Bag); Held Items that stay with their Pokémon card until it is Released, and Consumed Items. Seven placeholder Items as data.
- **Step 12** — Card names and art: the placeholder Pokémon, Supporters, Locations and Items take their real names (stats, effects, costs and copies unchanged), and card art from `assets/cards/<card id>.jpg` shows wherever a card appears: hand, arena, Trainer Spaces, Location spaces, Bag, PokéMart and selection screens. Cards without an image keep the placeholder look; new image files just work.
- **V1a** — One-screen layout (no rule changes): the game is drawn on a fixed 1920×1080 stage, scaled to fit any window from 1280×720 to 2560×1440, and the page never scrolls. Opponent bar on top; each side has Trainers at the back, then Locations, then Pokémon nearest the centre strip (Round, phase, turn, initiative, Weather). My hand fans along the bottom edge beside my info bar and Bag. On the right sit the prompt, a big main action button (Pass, Lock in, Keep hand…) and the PokéMart. The match log is a drawer, closed by default. Every card uses one layout in three fixed sizes (hand, board, mini), and hovering any card shows a large preview with every field, current stats, buffs and Held Item.
- **V1b** — Animations, and every opponent action made clear (no rule changes). Every action now also produces a list of structured events (card played, attack, damage, heal, buff, defeat, Power used, card moved, quest progress, Weather, phase changes, pass…) alongside the log. The screen keeps one element per card, so cards glide from hand to space, to piles, and to the Bag. The opponent acts one action at a time after a short thinking pause; a caption in the centre strip says each action in plain words; cards it plays are revealed large first (Supporters show what was paid and whether the Gift was met); attacks show an arrow and a lunge; damage, healing and buffs float as numbers; defeated cards fade and fly to the Released Pile; passes get a banner. Trainer reveals, mulligans and XP are shown together with a summary. Your own actions use the same animations, faster. Input is locked while actions play; click to skip to the end of one. Speed setting (⚙): Normal, Fast or Instant, remembered in this browser. Add `?autoplay` to the URL to watch the AI play both sides.
- **V2** — The warm tabletop look (no rule or animation changes). The stage is the wooden table from `assets/ui/table.jpg`, with the leather playmat as the arena: the mat is drawn in two halves so its tooled lines sit exactly on the zone boundaries and under the centre strip. Panels keep off the table's corner props (lamp, compass, book, scroll). One design system in `style.css` (CSS custom properties for colours, fonts, spacing, radii, shadows and the card sizes): wood, leather, parchment and brass, Cinzel for names and headings, Alegreya Sans for card text. Every card shares one face: name plate with a cost gem or coin (Card-Cost icons for Supporters), a large art window in a brass bevel, a type banner, parchment text, and stat badges with Attack/Strength/Speed icons and a rarity gem; each Card-Type has its own frame material. Inline SVG icons (no icon fonts). Brass buttons that press in, a glowing main button when it's your move, stitched spaces that glow as targets, card backs for hidden piles and choices. Alert glows; Spent cards dim and turn. All the art is preloaded before the first frame.
- **Step 13** — Passing gains Base Energy. Every Pass gives +1 Base Energy for the Round (spent before Exert, can go above 10, lost at the end of the Round), except the Pass that ends the Battle Phase, which gains nothing. The Pass button says which it will be ("Pass (+1 Energy)" or "Pass (end phase)"); the gain floats up from the Energy counter and shows in the caption. The AI now scores Pass alongside its other moves: worth more when one more Base Energy would let it play a card, weighed by the chance you pass straight back. Effect on game length (60 seeded AI-vs-AI matches): 16.1 → 19.2 actions per Battle Phase (17.2 → 19.4 counting stalled matches), 11.2 → 12.7 Rounds per match.
- **V3** — The hand, card interaction and board fit (no rule changes). My hand is a fanned arc at the bottom: every card shows at least its top half, the fan tightens as it grows, and a hovered card lifts straight up, full size, while its neighbours spread apart. Playable cards glow green-gold, unplayable ones dim, and a cost turns red when I can't pay it. Drag to play (click still works): the card follows the cursor with a tilt, valid destinations glow, and releasing elsewhere snaps it back; untargeted cards play by dropping them on the arena; Items can be dropped on a target or on my Bag; PokéMart Items are bought by dragging them to my Bag; every existing confirmation still appears. Commands, Trainer Powers and targeted cards use a curved brass targeting arrow: legal targets glow, others dim, and hovering one previews the result ("Misty −5", "Defeated"). Right-click or Escape cancels; right-click a card to inspect it with every keyword, Trigger Word, buff and Held Item explained. Bigger board cards (120×168) fill the mat, still lined up with its columns; zone labels are brass tabs on the centre strip, which now centres the Round and phase; the opponent's hand is a fan of card backs.
- **V4** — Combat and effect feedback, and readability (no rule changes). Every effect is driven by the action's events, one beat at a time in order. Attacks wind up, lunge along the targeting arrow's curve and spring back; on impact the table shakes a little (more for bigger hits), the target flashes and particles burst: dust and sparks for Physical attacks, an energy burst in the attacker's Type colour for Special ones; a trade lands on both cards at once; a Trainer hit shakes the Trainer card. Damage pops as a big red number on a splash while the Strength badge flashes and counts down; healing rises as green sparkles and counts up; buffs sweep a gold shimmer and pulse the stat badges; area damage (Hypervoice) sweeps across the side hitting each card in turn. Defeated cards flash, shake and burn away before flying to the Released Pile; a Trainer at 0 shatters, its zone dims and a banner says it has fallen; PokéDollars fly as coins to the counter. Summons land with a bounce and a dust ring (a flash for Quick Attack); Powers send a beam to their target; triggered abilities and Passives show a "Sewaddle · On Defend" tag; quest counters tick, a completed quest's XP flies to the XP counter; a Weather bracket switching on tints the board. Particles use one capped canvas; effects shorten at Fast, vanish at Instant, and "reduce motion" turns off shaking and thins particles. Readability: Spent cards keep their art (slight desaturation and turn, SPENT tag), board Attack and Strength are big bold numbers, and the Weather readout is icon, count and bracket pips (effects in the tooltip). The engine now also reports which ability or Passive triggered (an `abilityTriggered` event; no rule changes).
- **V5** — Turn flow and clarity (no rule changes). Banners sweep across the mat for each Round (with who has initiative), Breakfast, Battle and Supper (each with its own icon and colour), "Battle ends" when both players pass, "Your Turn" when it's my move and a smaller "Opponent's Turn" when it isn't; they shorten at Fast and vanish at Instant. The main action is a large brass medallion beside my side of the board that glows and pulses when it's my move; its label always says what will happen ("Pass · +1 Energy", "Pass · end Battle", "Lock in Trainers", "Keep hand", "Mulligan 2", "Confirm XP") and Space presses it. Passing while something is still playable shows a brief ring around it. On my turn, Alert Pokémon, Trainers with a usable Power and usable Bag Items pulse gently. Energy is shown as crystals (Base Energy gold, Exert blue); hovering or dragging anything that costs Energy previews what it would spend (Base crystals flicker, Exert glows amber with "+1 Exhaust"), or why it can't be paid. A dot beside each player's name shows whose move it is, and a "Passed" tag shows who passed last turn. A strip down the left of the mat shows the last 10 actions as card thumbnails, coloured by player, with the full description on hover. In Trainer placement, the mulligan and XP, the prompt says both players are choosing at once, and "Opponent ready ✓" appears once the AI locks in (it now takes a moment and locks in by itself, without waiting for you).
- **Step 14** — Passing with unmatched speed spends those Pokémon. When a player Passes (except the Pass that ends the Battle Phase) while only they have Active Pokémon at the top Speed-Tier, all of their Active Pokémon at that tier become Spent, and Alert is worked out again (the next tier down may become Alert, on either side). The +1 Base Energy is still gained. Before such a Pass you get a warning ("Passing now will make Pidgey and Absol Spent this Round, because the opponent can't match their speed.") with Pass anyway / Cancel, those Pokémon are highlighted, and the button says "Pass (+1 Energy, spends 2 Pokémon)". When it happens, a "Spent: unmatched speed" tag appears on each and the caption says so. The AI counts the attacks it would lose and almost always Commands first (the rule fired on 0.9% of passes in AI-vs-AI play). 60 seeded AI-vs-AI matches: 19.41 → 20.23 actions per Battle Phase (19.18 → 19.13 for finished matches), stalled 3 → 6; over 200 matches, stalled 13 → 16.
- **Step 15** — Stronger Pokémon (balance). Every placeholder Pokémon gets +1 Attack and +2 Strength on its printed stats; nothing else changes (the full list is in the rulebook). New balance report: `node tools/balance.js [matches]`. Before and after, 120 seeded AI-vs-AI matches:

  | | Before | After |
  |---|---|---|
  | Won · drawn · stalled | 87.5% · 5.0% · 7.5% | 99.2% · 0.0% · 0.8% |
  | Rounds per match (all · finished only) | 19.0 · 12.5 | 11.1 · 10.3 |
  | Actions per match (all · finished only) | 388 · 246 | 210 · 196 |
  | A player ran out of Pokémon (hand, Draw and Discard Piles) | 50.0%, at Round 11.1 | 20.8%, at Round 10.9 |
  | In wins: loser's Pokémon left · winner's Trainer Strength kept | 3.3 · 81.8% | 5.5 · 72.9% |
  | Damage per match: to Trainers · to Pokémon | 116 · 239 | 84 · 204 |

Add `?seed=123` to the URL to replay the same shuffle and coin flip.

## Balance report

After any balance change, run `node tools/balance.js [matches]` (default 120). It plays the same seeded AI-vs-AI matches every time and prints the win, draw and stall rates, match length, how often a player runs out of Pokémon, how close wins are, and damage dealt to Trainers vs Pokémon, so before and after can be compared directly.
